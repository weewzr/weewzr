## The problem

My CN5190 essay found that the viability of ammonia cracking depends on several linked quantities at once: equilibrium conversion, catalyst activity at low temperature, the heat available to the endothermic reaction, hydrogen recovery and purity, and the cost basis used. Reported costs differ partly because studies draw their system boundaries differently. AmCraX lets those quantities be varied together on one stated basis. The full argument is in the [essay](../../research/cn5190-ammonia-cracking/).

## Objectives

AmCraX answers questions of the kind the essay raises. How do operating temperature and pressure change conversion and the heat duty? What does heat recovery save? How do ammonia price and operating conditions change the cost of hydrogen on a consistent boundary? Every published number is traceable to a stored result file.

The [public site](https://weewzr.github.io/AmCraX/) shows the published results. The [repository](https://github.com/weewzr/AmCraX) holds the code, the Aspen Plus model files, the results, the [technical report](https://weewzr.github.io/AmCraX/downloads/AmCraX_Technical_Report.pdf) and the pitch deck.

## Architecture

- **Aspen Plus V15 flowsheet.** Liquid ammonia is pumped, vaporised, preheated against the reactor effluent and cracked at 600 °C and 20 bar. The product is cooled and purified, and the tail gas is burned for heat. The property method is Peng-Robinson.
- **Automation.** Python generates each Aspen input file from a typed case definition and runs Aspen Plus through its COM interface, one instance at a time.
- **Independent Python models.** They cover thermochemistry, equilibrium, the process, heat integration, kinetics, costs and carbon intensity, and they check the Aspen results.
- **Platform.** A FastAPI service and a React dashboard with a React Flow process diagram. It runs locally next to Aspen Plus.
- **Public site.** A static build of the stored results on GitHub Pages. It never contacts Aspen Plus or a server. Its equilibrium calculator runs an ideal-gas model in the browser and is labelled as such.

## Approach

The baseline case feeds 100 kmol/h of ammonia. Aspen Plus gives an ammonia conversion of 98.1% and 251.6 kg/h of hydrogen. The feed and effluent exchanger recovers 612 kW, which lowers the external heating from 2,905 kW to 2,293 kW.

The equilibrium results were checked like for like against the independent Python model. The largest difference in conversion is 0.045 percentage points with ideal-gas properties in both models and 0.185 percentage points with Peng-Robinson in both.

The validation matrix has 137 items: 105 pass, 22 fail and 10 are informational. Twenty of the failures are flowsheet cross-checks where the two models use different property models in the feed section. Two are kinetics curves reproduced within about 0.11 against a tolerance of 0.05.

The preliminary levelised cost of hydrogen is 3.92 USD/kg at an ammonia price of 450 USD/t, and the ammonia feed is 3.04 USD/kg of that. Ten optimised designs were re-solved in Aspen Plus. All converged, and their primary results agree with the Python model within 1.15%.

## What it does not cover

- It is not a validated digital twin. No plant data were used.
- Steady state only.
- The Aspen reactor is an equilibrium block. The kinetics were fitted at 1 atm and are extrapolated to 20 bar.
- The purification step is a component splitter. The product purity of 99.96 mol% follows from the assumed split and is below the limit for fuel-cell hydrogen. Ammonia polishing is not modelled.
- The costs are a preliminary estimate, below published values of 5.1 to 6.4 USD/kg at comparable ammonia prices. The model omits NOx treatment, ammonia polishing, compression and installed-cost factors.
- The carbon intensity is a simplified account, not a life-cycle assessment.
- The public site does not run Aspen Plus.
