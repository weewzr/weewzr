## The problem

My CN5190 essay found that the viability of ammonia cracking depends on several linked quantities at once: equilibrium conversion, catalyst activity at low temperature, the heat available to the endothermic reaction, hydrogen recovery and purity, and the cost basis used. Reported costs differ partly because studies draw their system boundaries differently. AmCraX lets those quantities be varied together on one stated basis. The full argument is in the [essay](../../research/cn5190-ammonia-cracking/).

## Objectives

AmCraX answers questions of the kind the essay raises. How do operating temperature and pressure change conversion and the heat duty? What does heat recovery save? How do ammonia price and operating conditions change the cost of hydrogen on a consistent boundary? Every published number is traceable to a stored result file.

The [public site](https://weewzr.github.io/AmCraX/) opens on the process simulator and shows the published results. The [repository](https://github.com/weewzr/AmCraX) holds the code, the Aspen Plus model files, the results, the [technical report](https://weewzr.github.io/AmCraX/downloads/AmCraX_Technical_Report.pdf) and the pitch deck.

## Architecture

- **Aspen Plus V15 flowsheet.** Liquid ammonia is pumped, vaporised, preheated against the reactor effluent and cracked at 600 °C and 20 bar. The product is cooled and purified, and the tail gas is burned for heat. The property method is Peng-Robinson.
- **Automation.** Python generates each Aspen input file from a typed case definition and runs Aspen Plus through its COM interface, one instance at a time.
- **Independent Python models.** They cover thermochemistry, equilibrium, the process, heat integration, kinetics, purification, costs and carbon intensity, and they check the Aspen results.
- **Platform.** A FastAPI service and a React dashboard with a React Flow process diagram. It runs locally next to Aspen Plus.
- **Public site.** A static build of the stored results on GitHub Pages. It never contacts Aspen Plus or a server. Its first page is a process simulator: where an input matches a stored case it shows the stored Aspen Plus result, and elsewhere it uses a browser port of the Python models. Each value says which of the two it is. The animation shows flow direction only and is not a transient simulation.

## Approach

The baseline case feeds 100 kmol/h of ammonia. Aspen Plus gives an ammonia conversion of 98.1% and 251.6 kg/h of hydrogen. The feed and effluent exchanger recovers 612 kW, which lowers the external heating from 2,905 kW to 2,293 kW.

Phase 2 moved every research result onto one reference scenario: itemised capital cost in 2024 USD, 8000 operating hours per year, a 10% discount rate over 25 years, and grey ammonia at 450 USD/t.

## Results

On the reference scenario the baseline levelised cost of hydrogen is 4.015 USD/kg (5.174 SGD/kg), of which the ammonia feed is 3.040 USD/kg. The earlier figure of 3.92 USD/kg comes from the legacy plant-level cost correlation. It is kept as a stored core result and is not the basis of any research result. Ten optimised designs were re-solved in Aspen Plus. All converged, and their primary results agree with the Python model within 1.15%.

Four hypotheses from the essay are tested for Singapore: end use, ammonia feedstock, catalyst and heat integration, and purification. Each verdict follows a documented rule (version 2) that was fixed before the analyses, and is printed with its envelope beside the version 1 verdict. The verdicts are kept in the [evidence matrix](https://github.com/weewzr/AmCraX/blob/main/docs/research/evidence_matrix.md), which the AmCraX README names as the reference if any summary differs from it. They describe model results and are not experimental validation.

## Verification

AmCraX keeps four kinds of evidence apart: numerical verification, comparison of the Python models with Aspen Plus, comparison with published literature, and independent experimental validation. It has the first three and none of the fourth.

The equilibrium results were checked like for like against the independent Python model over a 168-point grid. The largest difference in conversion is 0.045 percentage points with ideal-gas properties in both models and 0.185 percentage points with Peng-Robinson in both.

The validation matrix has 137 items: 105 pass, 22 fail and 10 are informational. Twenty of the failures are flowsheet cross-checks where the two models use different property models in the feed section; they keep their 2% tolerance. Two are kinetics prediction items, where the published curves are reproduced only approximately.

The browser simulator is a port of the Python models. It agrees with them on 82 stored test vectors to about 1e-7. This is numerical verification of the port, recorded in `results/research/simulator/parity_report.json`.

## What it does not cover

- It is not a validated digital twin. No plant data were used, and there is no independent experimental validation.
- Steady state only.
- The Aspen reactor is an equilibrium block, so conversion is an upper bound. No rate law is validated at 20 bar.
- The purification step is a reduced-order splitter with its recovery as an input. The product purity of 99.961 mol% follows from the assumed split and is below the 99.97% limit quoted for fuel-cell hydrogen. Ammonia polishing is not modelled.
- The reference capital cost comes from one paper's itemised table. No supplier price exists for any cracking catalyst.
- The carbon intensity is a simplified account, not a life-cycle assessment. Under the actual Singapore carbon tax the tax is zero for every route; any price on embodied emissions is hypothetical.
- The public site does not run Aspen Plus or Python.
